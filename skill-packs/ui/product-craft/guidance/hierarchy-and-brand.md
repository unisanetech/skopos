# Hierarchy, Archetype, And Brand

## Diagnose Before Styling

1. Identify the primary job, risk, frequency, density, and decision evidence.
2. Name the surface archetype: operational workbench, transaction, discovery,
   documentation, marketplace, public service, editorial, or expressive marketing.
3. Inspect bound tokens, components, assets, nearby workflows, and approved precedents.
4. For design-significant work, capture same-task references with URL, viewport, state,
   date, and supported decision. A homepage is not evidence for a dense tool or transaction.
5. Treat missing art direction as a gap; do not fill it with AI or admin-dashboard defaults.

## Compose For The Job

Set purpose, reading path, content edge, navigation, density, and disclosure before
decoration. Workbenches may need dense lists and inspectors; transactions lead with
amounts and consequences; discovery coordinates filters and views; documentation pairs
explanation with code.

Express product character through real domain objects, workflows, language, and bound
assets—not an accent color applied to an interchangeable shell.

Do not default to a dark bar, tinted canvas, permanent left navigation, carded list, and
permanent right inspector. Decide what persists, becomes contextual, or serves the
primary task edge. Treat familiar admin composition as an unproven hypothesis.

## Budget Attention And Color

1. Use one neutral family for canvas, surfaces, text, and structure.
2. Use one interactive accent family by default. Add saturated color only for a
   semantic, data, or user-authored distinction with a named job.
3. Do not color owner, recency, category, activity, priority, navigation, and actions
   simultaneously. Routine status should recede; exceptional risk may demand emphasis.
4. Pair color meaning with text or another non-color cue.
5. Inventory every saturated background, border, icon, badge, dot, link, and colored
   text region visible at once. Remove color until the primary action, current
   selection, and urgent exception regain a clear order.

Reject decoration that compensates for weak hierarchy, unexplained local tokens,
generic gradient-card styling, or references from a different product purpose.
